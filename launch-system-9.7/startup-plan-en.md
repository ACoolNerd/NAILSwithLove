# Nails with Love by Yesenia
## English Startup Plan — Home Boutique + Mobile Nail Service, Cali, Colombia

Prepared July 13, 2026. This is a researched planning document, not legal, tax, investment, or medical advice. Confirm business activity codes, tax obligations, home-use permissions, sanitation requirements, and investment contracts with the relevant authorities and licensed Colombian professionals before launch.

## 1. Executive decision

Launch as a **home-based boutique with mobile appointments**, using the personal brand **Nails with Love by Yesenia**. Start lean, document every sale and cost, and delay leasing a storefront until the business demonstrates at least three consecutive months of demand near practical capacity.

Brand promise: **“Nails made with love, in the comfort of your home.”**

Primary market: women in Cali seeking convenience, privacy, clear pricing, appointment reliability, hygienic practices, and personalized designs.

Recommended legal sequence:

1. Confirm Yesenia’s right to work and identification documentation.
2. Verify the residential property’s permitted economic activity before serving clients there.
3. Start as a registered natural-person merchant if she is self-funding and operating alone.
4. Obtain RUT/NIT, commercial registration, municipal tax guidance, sanitary guidance, and business financial accounts.
5. Convert to or form an **S.A.S. before selling equity or admitting shareholders**.

## 2. Formalization roadmap

### Step 1 — Name and activity check

- Search the business name in RUES and the Cali Chamber of Commerce.
- Reserve social handles and a matching domain.
- Ask the Chamber/DIAN to confirm the correct CIIU activities for manicure, pedicure, nail extensions, retail products, mobile service, and any future education services.

### Step 2 — Home-use and property approval

- Use Cali’s free online land-use consultation for the exact property and proposed activity.
- Review the lease, condominium/HOA rules, landlord permission, client traffic, noise, waste, parking, and signage restrictions.
- A residential address should not be advertised as a customer location until permitted.

### Step 3 — RUT and tax profile

- Register or update the RUT through DIAN.
- Discuss ordinary income tax versus the optional SIMPLE regime with a Colombian accountant. Do not choose solely from an online summary.
- Determine electronic invoicing duties, IVA status, ICA obligations, withholding treatment, and document-support requirements.

### Step 4 — Commercial registration

- Register Yesenia as a natural-person merchant and, if applicable, the establishment name with the Cali Chamber of Commerce.
- Renew commercial registration annually by the legal deadline.
- 2026 fees depend on declared assets. The Chamber lists COP 48,400 for an establishment in its lowest asset band, plus merchant registration calculated under the published formula, RUES form and certificates.

### Step 5 — Cali municipal obligations

- Ask the City about ICA/RIT registration, opening communication, land use, signage, fire/risk requirements, and home-based operations.
- Keep the economic activity on the municipal record consistent with the Chamber registration.

### Step 6 — Sanitation and professional documentation

- Follow Resolution 2117 of 2010 and the biosecurity manual under Resolution 2827 of 2006 for ornamental beauty/manicure operations.
- Maintain training evidence, biosecurity protocol, cleaning/disinfection logs, equipment maintenance, product labels/NSO verification, waste plan, clean/dirty separation, handwashing access and single-use controls.
- Request Cali health guidance and any inspection/concept applicable to the specific model.
- Do not perform medical procedures or work on suspected infection, open wounds, severe inflammation or conditions requiring a health professional.

### Step 7 — Investor readiness

- A sole proprietorship has no shares to sell. If the plan requires equity investors, form an **S.A.S.**, adopt statutes, document capitalization, open a company bank account, and use a Colombian lawyer/accountant.
- A loan or revenue-share agreement also requires written terms, risk disclosure, tax review and an aggregate payment cap.

## 3. Recommended financial and software stack

### Banking and payments

**Launch recommendation:** Nequi Negocios for QR and Tap to Phone, plus a separate account dedicated to the business. Nequi’s official July 2026 information lists QR Negocios at no charge, Tap to Phone at 1.99% + IVA for Visa/Mastercard, and payment-link rates that vary by payment rail. Card transactions may also generate tax withholding.

**Growth option:** Bold Payments + Bold business account, or a Bancolombia business/current savings account with Wompi. Compare settlement, withholding, extracts, account certification and support before switching.

Accept: cash, Bre-B/QR, Nequi, bank transfer, payment link, Visa/Mastercard contactless, and documented deposits. Never accept a screenshot as proof; verify the transaction in the provider app.

### Accounting

- **Best Colombia-first choice:** Alegra for DIAN-compatible electronic invoicing and local accounting workflows. Its official Colombia plan page lists a starter electronic-invoicing plan from COP 17,900/month at the time researched.
- **QuickBooks Online:** globally available and supports Colombian tax-rate configuration, expense capture and reports, but it is subscription-priced in USD and is not a substitute for a Colombian electronic invoicing provider or local accountant. Use QuickBooks only if the accountant confirms the workflow.
- Practical setup: Alegra + accountant for compliance; Treinta or the booking system for daily mobile sales/inventory. Avoid duplicating the same transaction manually in three systems.

### CRM and booking

- **Start:** WhatsApp Business + Google Calendar + Treinta customer records.
- **Upgrade:** AgendaPro Colombia when automated scheduling, CRM, reminders, payments and marketing justify the cost.
- **Alternative:** Fresha offers a one-person plan and automated communications, but verify Colombian payment support and total foreign-currency costs before adopting.

Required customer fields: name, mobile, consent status, source, first/last visit, service, nail condition notes, sensitivities disclosed by client, preferred colors/shapes, spend, deposit, next-maintenance date, referral code and photo consent.

## 4. Home boutique layout

Use a 2.4m × 2.4m minimum dedicated area if allowed, with:

- wipeable manicure desk positioned near ventilation;
- technician and client ergonomic chairs;
- task lighting and certified UV/LED lamp;
- dust extractor for enhancement services;
- closed, labeled storage for clean tools and products;
- physically separate container for used tools;
- handwashing or immediately accessible sink;
- lidded waste bins and sharps handling appropriate to the procedures used;
- polish wall/storage away from heat and direct sun;
- photo corner with neutral background and consistent lighting;
- visible pricing, policies, QR payment and booking stand;
- fire extinguisher and first-aid kit appropriate to the space.

Workflow: reception/consultation → hand hygiene → service → photo with consent → payment verified → aftercare → workstation reset and documented disinfection.

## 5. Services to offer

Launch only services supported by Yesenia’s training and product system.

- classic manicure and pedicure;
- semipermanent/gel manicure and pedicure;
- rubber base or builder overlay;
- structured gel/BIAB-style reinforcement where trained;
- Gel X/soft-gel tips;
- acrylic full sets and maintenance;
- polygel/acrygel where trained;
- French, baby boomer, chrome, cat-eye, aura, ombré and seasonal art;
- repairs, removals, length/shape upgrades;
- hand/foot hydration add-ons that remain cosmetic and non-medical;
- bridal/event, group and corporate bookings;
- custom press-ons as a later retail product.

Do not advertise treatment for fungus, ingrown nails or other medical conditions.

## 9. Break-even strategy

Planning assumptions:

- average ticket target: COP 115,000;
- product cost: 18% of sales;
- payment cost allowance: 2.5% of sales;
- average travel: COP 12,000/appointment;
- fixed monthly overhead: COP 1,750,000;
- owner compensation target: COP 2,200,000/month before personal tax.

Contribution per average appointment ≈ COP 79,425.

- Operating break-even: about **23 appointments/month** or **6/week**.
- Break-even plus owner compensation: about **50 appointments/month** or **12/week**.
- Practical capacity at 3 appointments/day × 22 days: 66/month; protect quality and travel time.

Weekly control: inquiries, deposits, completed appointments, average ticket, add-on rate, no-shows, product cost, travel, rebooking, referrals, gross margin and cash balance.

## 10. Loyalty and referral plan

**Love Points:** 1 point per COP 1,000 spent. At 400 points, issue COP 20,000 service credit. Credits expire after 90 days, are not cash, cannot combine with another promotion, and exclude deposits/travel.

**Referral:** after a new referred client completes and pays for the appointment, referrer and new client each receive COP 15,000 credit for a future service.

**Maintenance:** offer priority booking, not an automatic permanent discount. Use a six-visit milestone reward such as free simple art or hydration add-on.
